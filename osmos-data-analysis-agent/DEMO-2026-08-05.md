## 1. What this is

An agent that works marketplace ad-performance tickets. You describe the problem in normal
language. It picks the right runbook, pulls the data, does the maths itself, and stops to
check with you at each step instead of handing you a finished essay.

**The most important thing: it never writes SQL.**

All 43 reports are SQL templates that live in kamService and have been through code review.
The agent only picks *which columns, which dates, which filters*. It cannot change the SQL.
So if a number looks wrong, you can go read the query in a pull request — the agent can't
have invented it.

```
you ask a question in plain language
        ↓
Claude Code picks ONE of 11 runbooks, by matching what you asked
        ↓
the reporting server runs a frozen report
        ↓
kamService  →  BigQuery
```

**What a "runbook" is:** a written procedure for one kind of problem — one for CTR, one for
ROAS, one for response rate, and so on. Each one says which report to pull first, what to
check, and when to stop. The agent follows it rather than improvising.

## 3. All 15 tickets, in detail

### Ticket 1 — "Active campaign not serving ads" · vegolution · bigbasket

**What they said:** 8 targeted keywords aren't serving. Only "chapathi" gets responses.
Campaign `Paratha`, ACTIVE, created 13 July.

**What it pulled:** the campaign's keyword list, per-keyword performance, marketplace-wide
request volume, and who else serves those brand terms.

**What it found — the complaint was correct, but for three different reasons at once.**

Over 13–15 July there were only ~423 impressions in total. "chapathi" alone took 82% of
impressions and 91% of spend.

Here is why each keyword behaved as it did. A keyword needs **100 requests in 7 days**
before the system will map it to a category. Below that, it cannot receive any responses.

| Keyword | Requests | Responses | Fill rate | Eligible? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| chapathi | 9,615 | 7,896 | 82.1% | yes |
| malabar paratha | 4,069 | 3,465 | 85.2% | yes |
| tempeh | 2,894 | 1,416 | 48.9% | yes |
| hello tempayy | 1,026 | 560 | 54.6% | yes |
| protein batter | 93 | 25 | 26.9% | **no — below threshold** |
| protein butter | 61 | 24 | 39.3% | **no — below threshold** |
| protein paratha | 43 | 18 | 41.9% | **no — below threshold** |
| protein chapathi | 32 | 16 | 30.4% | **no — below threshold** |

**Three separate causes, reported as one ticket:**

| Cause | Detail |
|---|---|
| 1. Too few searches | Four keywords are below the 100-request threshold, so no category mapping exists. Working as designed |
| 2. Competing with themselves | `tempeh` and `hello tempayy` **are** serving — but the advertiser's own "Search Only momos" campaign takes **79.0%** of the impressions, leaving `Paratha` 19.7% |
| 3. Thin supply | The rest serve normally. Low fill just means Vegolution is the only advertiser eligible to answer |

**A typo the ticket never mentioned:** the campaign targets **"protein batter"**, not
"protein butter".

**One genuine platform finding, passed to the relevancy team:** on the brand search *hello
tempayy*, about **59%** of filled slots go to *other* advertisers — 29.5% to Aashirvaad
flatbreads and 29.2% to dairy paneer products, both getting almost no clicks. A specific
relevance cache is doing this, which is where the relevancy team should start.

### Ticket 2 — "Low CTR on custom, category and home pages" · apollo

**What they said:** CTR is down on three page types, comparing March with June.

**What it found — nothing is broken. Impressions grew far faster than clicks.**

CTR is clicks ÷ impressions. If impressions grow and clicks don't grow as fast, CTR falls
even though nothing is wrong.

| Page | March impressions | June impressions | Impressions Δ | Clicks Δ | March CTR | June CTR |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| PRODUCT | 336,496 | 2,718,392 | **+708%** | +23.6% | 5.96% | 0.91% |
| HOME | 305,099 | 1,081,181 | +254% | **−34.5%** | 1.48% | 0.27% |
| CATEGORY | 181,592 | 366,440 | +102% | +17.6% | 2.46% | 1.43% |
| CUSTOM | 2,773,143 | 3,067,623 | +10.6% | −46.9% | 0.611% | 0.293% |
| SEARCH | 4,613,264 | 4,327,115 | −6.2% | −13.3% | 1.490% | 1.377% |
| TPA *(new)* | — | 313,312 | new | new | — | **6.92%** |
| **TOTAL** | **8,209,594** | **11,874,063** | **+44.6%** | **+7.5%** | 1.397% | 1.038% |

**The check that answered their real worry.** Their underlying fear was double-counted
impressions. The agent checked the ratio of impressions to responses — how often an ad we
returned actually got displayed:

| Page | March | June |
|---|---|---|
| PRODUCT | 13.8% | **98.1%** |
| HOME | 3.0% | 6.4% |
| CATEGORY | 14.8% | 28.2% |
| CUSTOM | 57.3% | 75.1% |

In March only about 1 in 7 returned ads actually rendered on the product page. In June
nearly all of them do. If we were double-counting, this number would go **above 100%**. It
sits at 98.1%. **So there is no double counting and no pipeline defect** — Apollo changed
how the product page renders.

**Three causes, all on Apollo's side:**

| Cause | Evidence |
|---|---|
| Product page rendering change | The 13.8% → 98.1% jump above |
| Catalogue re-categorisation | "Health Devices" went from 86,348 impressions to 0; "Medical Equipments & Devices" appeared. A whole clinical taxonomy (Cardiology, Dentistry, Orthopedics…) was added with zero impressions. Health & Nutrition went from **56 to 8,703 merchants** |
| Advertiser turnover | New merchants brought 1,045,910 impressions at **0.61%** CTR. Departed merchants had been at **3.96%** — that's **16% of all March clicks, gone** |

CENTRUM alone added +749,612 impressions, about 20% of the whole increase, at half its
previous CTR. Separately, TPA is the best page on the marketplace and actually *lifts* the
blended number.

**A data-quality bug spotted on the way:** the new taxonomy contains a genuine duplicate —
"Ear Nose & Oropharynx" and "Ear, Nose & Oropharynx" are two separate rows with identical
values.

### Ticket 3 — "Multiple SKUs on 'Green Tea' not serving" · bigbasket

**What they said:** SKUs in campaign `FF_Green tea` aren't serving for "green tea". They
asked whether the keyword→category mapping was broken.

**What it found — nothing is broken. All 12 SKUs are serving.** 214,648 impressions, 5,300
clicks, INR 5,830 spent.

**How it answered the mapping question without being able to read the mapping.** We have no
report that lists keyword→category mappings. So it reasoned backwards: SKUs from *both*
`beverages>tea>tea-bags` (9 SKUs) and `beverages>tea>green-tea` (3 SKUs) served through the
targeted-keyword path. That can only happen if the keyword is mapped to both. **Proof by
delivery.**

| What the ticket claimed | What the data shows |
|---|---|
| The mapping is wrong | No — proven by delivery through the targeted path |
| Only SKU 40092352 is serving | No — it's **8th of 12** (6,968 impressions vs 44,964 for the top one) |
| Competing brands are absent | No — Girnar has 117,712, Organic India ~11,800, plus Tetley, Society, Tea Culture |
| We're under-delivering | No — about **60% share of voice** |

**Why it looked broken to them:** a search page only shows a few ad slots. Checking by eye
shows one or two SKUs, which is normal.

**A useful finding for the brand:** the three actual *green tea* SKUs have the **best**
engagement (7.75%, 5.61%, 9.68% CTR) but the **fewest** impressions. The volume is going to
tulsi and chamomile SKUs at 1.17–2.34%. Worth splitting into its own campaign.

### Ticket 4 — "Centrum Ostocalcium keyword CPC issue" · apollo

**What they said:** a table implying competitors are pushing up CPC on "centrum".

**What it found — the table was wrong. All four rows are Centrum's own campaigns.**

| Row in their table | What it actually is |
|---|---|
| Centrum EXACT · ₹304.42 | Centrum's own brand-defence campaign |
| **Supradyn EXACT · ₹61.40** | **Centrum's own campaign, bidding on a rival's brand name** |
| **Revital EXACT · ₹64.70** | **Same Centrum campaign, bidding on another rival's name** |
| Zincovit EXACT · ₹0.00 | Same campaign, no delivery |

The table compares defending your own brand against attacking someone else's — two
different auctions, both theirs. It also compares an *achieved average CPC* against a *bid*,
which are different things.

**What competitors actually pay on "centrum":**

| Advertiser | Impressions | Clicks | Spend | CPC |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **CENTRUM** | **6,710** | **304** | **₹51,550.85** | ₹169.58 |
| HEALTHKART | 3,548 | 36 | ₹2,480.00 | ₹68.89 |
| Diataal | 1,893 | 13 | ₹495.00 | ₹38.08 |
| Supradyn | 675 | 17 | ₹679.73 | ₹39.98 |

Centrum holds **47.3% of impressions, 80.9% of clicks and 93.2% of spend.** Only four
external campaigns target "centrum" deliberately, at **₹11.01, ₹11.06, ₹15.00 and ₹0** per
click. **Nobody is outbidding them.**

**The real cause — they are bidding against themselves.** "centrum" is targeted in **eight**
Centrum campaigns, and **six were created 6–13 July**, right before the ticket.

In this kind of auction the *second* highest bidder sets the price you pay. Their second
highest bidder is their own sibling campaign bidding ₹300+. External rivals bid ₹11–15.
So lowering the bid doesn't save money — **it just hands the slot to another Centrum
campaign.**

### Ticket 5 — "Managed service performance improvement" · firstcry

**What they said:** ROAS is below the 2.5–5 target for two brands, even after optimisation.
(ROAS = revenue ÷ ad spend.)

**What it found — one brand has no problem, and the other's target is impossible.**

**CUGO:** their PLA ROAS is **2.49 — inside target.** The reported 1.79 is a blended number
dragged down by a Display campaign that spent ₹5,769 and returned nothing.

**Ashpveda:** 115 products advertised, ₹28,952 spent, ₹14,373 returned, 33 orders.

| Finding | Number |
|---|---|
| SKUs producing **zero** orders | **97 of 115 (84%)** |
| Spend those SKUs consumed | ₹17,542 — **60.6% of total** |
| Four worst categories | ₹10,590 spent, ₹668 returned — ROAS **0.06** |

**The optimisation work did succeed** — click-to-order doubled, order value rose 20%,
conversion improved. ROAS stayed low because CPC rose 64% while CTR halved.

**The arithmetic that actually answers the ticket:**

| Scenario | Result |
|---|---|
| Keep every SKU with ROAS ≥ 1.0 (perfect hindsight) | ROAS **1.92** |
| Keep only SKUs with ROAS ≥ 2.0 | ROAS **3.33 — but on only ₹1,811 over five weeks** (~1.5% of the ₹100K budget) |
| Ashpveda's **total** site revenue, all channels | **₹19,114** — against ₹28,952 of ad spend |

Ads already drive about 75% of this brand's revenue on FirstCry. Hitting ROAS 2.5 on
₹100K/month would need ₹250,000/month of attributed sales — roughly **15× the brand's
entire demand on the platform.**

**So the target and the budget cannot both be met.** Spending more only pushes CPC higher
and CTR lower.

### Ticket 6 — "New campaigns spend their budget too fast" · firstcry

**What they said:** they believe new campaigns are prioritised and burn budget too quickly —
₹10k in 4 hours.

**What it found — it's a setting, and the evidence reverses their theory.**

Both named campaigns are set to **ACCELERATED** delivery, which tells the system to spend
the daily budget as fast as it can, with no pacing. ₹10k in 4 hours is exactly what that
setting does.

Then it sampled 120 of 1,666 active campaigns, grouped by ID as a rough proxy for age:

| Group | Sampled | ACCELERATED | STANDARD | % accelerated |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Oldest** | 40 | **28** | 12 | **70.0%** |
| Middle | 40 | 25 | 15 | 62.5% |
| **Newest** | 40 | **0** | **40** | **0.0%** |

**Every single one of the 40 newest campaigns is STANDARD.** If new campaigns were being
prioritised, they'd be the fast spenders. The opposite is true.

**And the campaign they complained about isn't new.** It sits in the *oldest* group. It was
**restarted**, not created — which explains the pattern: restarted campaigns carry the old
ACCELERATED default from when they were first set up.

### Ticket 8 — "Category page has the most requests but the worst response rate" · apollo

**What they said:** no client list, no customer ID, empty body. Matched to Apollo because it
came from the same person and queue as ticket 7, about an hour apart — then confirmed
because the data matches the described symptom exactly.

**What it found — this isn't a fill problem, it's a tagging problem.**

**92.2% of category-page requests arrive with no page name and no category attached.**
Nothing can be matched to a request that doesn't say what it's for, so those requests can
never be filled.

| Measure | Value |
|---|---|
| Category page share of requests | **41.6%** |
| Category page share of responses | **2.5%** |
| Category page share of spend | 3.6% |
| Headline response rate | 1.36% |
| **Response rate on correctly tagged requests** | **~55.6% — better than SEARCH, PRODUCT or TPA** |

So the bad headline number is entirely an artefact of the 92.2% that arrive untagged.
**This is one of the three genuine platform findings.**

### Ticket 9 — "Improve BU and RR; is L2 fallback enabled?" · tira

**What they said:** three questions — how to improve budget utilisation, how to improve
response rate, and whether category fallback is on. Advisory, not a fault report. No dates.

**What it found — there aren't enough eligible advertisers to fill the demand.**

| Measure | Value |
|---|---|
| Budget vs actual spend | Budget is **3.6× spend** |
| Campaigns with an empty wallet | **1 of 1,876** |
| Impact of L2 fallback | Would affect **0.5%** of requests — immaterial |

Money is not the constraint. Low budget utilisation and low response rate are the same
problem seen from two directions: not enough advertisers are eligible to answer the

(2,664,012 × 14 ≈ 37.3M, matching the 38.4M total budget) and used the total-budget column
instead. It also noticed the utilisation column is a ratio, not a percentage.

**What it found — a marketplace-wide increase in impressions, and the dates are ~2 weeks

KFC explains about 59% of the lost revenue but **essentially none of the CTR/CPC change.**
The old figures were struck through in the log rather than deleted.

### Ticket 11 — "80 campaigns stuck under 50% budget utilisation" · bigbasket

**What they said:** an internal spreadsheet listing 80 campaigns below 50% BU.

**First problem — the spreadsheet's "Campaign ID" column contained two different kinds of
ID.** 58 rows held a campaign *group* ID; the other 22 held a campaign ID. Looking all 80 up
as campaign IDs **silently returned only 22 rows, with no error.** The agent noticed the
count, re-looked-up the missing ones as group IDs, and recovered them.

**Second problem — 8 of the 80 were false positives** already running at 100% BU.

**The real cause — the campaigns have almost nothing to sell.**

| Finding | Number |
|---|---|
| Campaigns containing **exactly one** SKU | **47 of 80** |
| Campaigns with 2 or fewer SKUs | 62 of 80 |
| Campaigns with an empty wallet | 2 of 80 |
| Recoverable spend | **INR 399,058/day** |
| Blended return of this group | **38×** (46,119 spend → 1,760,687 attributed sales) |

A campaign holding one product can only win the auctions where that product is the right
answer. No amount of budget changes that. The low response rate in these categories is the
*result* of thin supply, not the cause.

It also ruled out the alternatives: demand isn't short, and bids aren't the issue — in the
worst-affected categories there is only one campaign and one merchant, so there are no
rivals to outbid.

### Ticket 12 — "PLA ads not serving for FF_Snacks" · bigbasket

**What they said:** "snack" and "snacks" aren't responding. The client had already been told
to raise bids, which didn't help. They asked if the category-keyword mapping was broken.

**What it found — nothing is broken, and the mapping is fine. The complaint was true when
raised and stopped being true the same day.**

The campaign was six days old and had delivered 21 impressions total. It began serving
normally on **3 August — the day the ticket was raised.** Over 3–4 August: **11,820
impressions, 303 clicks, INR 19,808 spend, INR 233,423 attributed sales, 79% top-of-search.**

**Why it looked broken — three reports each showed something different:**

| Report | Showed | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Targeted keywords | **0 rows** | The campaign is automated with no manual keywords, so there's nothing to report |
| Search queries | 30,660 impressions, but 0 manual | 100% auto-matched |
| Responded SKUs | 25,626 impressions across 6 relevance caches | The serving layer matches "snacks" internally |

**And "snack" looked empty because the singular is matched by the plural keyword** — 997
impressions and INR 1,689.60 were sitting under the "snacks" row.

**The bid advice was unnecessary, and the draft reply says so:** "snack" clears at
INR 555–689 CPM against the campaign's achieved ~INR 1,676 — and the campaign is on
automatic bidding anyway, so the advertiser doesn't set the bid.

### Ticket 14 — "Low response rate despite more requests" · tira

**What they said:** two periods where requests rose but responses didn't. They also
referenced a chart image the agent never received.

**First, it corrected the dates.** Only **17–18 July** actually broke, not 16–19.

| Date | Requests | Responses | Response rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| Jul 16 | 867,673 | 595,037 | 68.58% ← normal |
| **Jul 17** | 1,668,528 | 621,230 | **37.23%** |
| **Jul 18** | 1,502,830 | 596,297 | **39.68%** |
| Jul 19 | 921,577 | 625,608 | 67.88% ← recovered |
| Jul 30 | 840,046 | 543,602 | 64.71% |
| **Jul 31** | 1,434,501 | 529,233 | **36.89%** |

**The decisive number — what the extra requests achieved:**

| Day | Extra requests | Extra responses |
|---|---|---|
| Jul 17 | +800,855 | +26,193 → only **3.3%** got filled |
| Jul 31 | +594,455 | **−14,369** → responses actually *fell* |

**It ruled out the obvious explanation.** If budgets were running out, you'd see a normal
morning and a sudden cliff. Instead the response rate was **24.66% at midnight** and still
23.35% at 11pm — bad all day, evenly. The request surge was also uniform across all 24
hours, including 3–5am when real shoppers aren't active. **Real demand doesn't behave like
that.**

**Then the actual cause, found by breaking it down by brand.** On 31 July, TIRA sent
**633,205 requests filtered to the brand `Anua` — 919× the previous day's 689 — and every
single one returned zero ads.**

| Check | Result |
|---|---|
| Anua's share of the request increase | **107.5%** (over 100% because every other brand's requests *fell* 17.9% that day) |
| Anua's share of all CUSTOM requests that day | **63%** |
| Response rate on 31 Jul **excluding Anua** | **60.70%** |
| Response rate on 30 Jul, same basis | **60.47%** |

**Remove Anua and the incident does not exist.**

**It's not a name-matching bug either** — Anua *is* in tira's catalogue with 24 SKUs. There
is simply no Anua campaign, so nothing can serve. **Still leaking ~20k requests a day.**

**A data-quality finding along the way:** **100% of TIRA's CUSTOM requests have a blank
category** — 1,005,400 out of 1,005,400 on 31 July. Category-level analysis is structurally
impossible for this marketplace, which is why the investigation had to go via hourly shape
and brand instead.

### Ticket 15 — "Increase in CPC for product and category pages" · takealot

**What they said:** one sentence. No dates, no numbers, no scope.

This one was pushed far beyond the runbook, over about twenty follow-up questions. **It is
the best demonstration of the agent under sustained pressure — it reversed itself seven
times, and every reversal came from digging deeper, not from being told it was wrong.**

**The verdict: half right, and pointed at the wrong thing.**

| Page | Week-over-week CPC | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| PRODUCT | 2.6507 → 2.5993 (**−1.9%**) | Real event, but on **4 August only** |
| CATEGORY | 4.3080 → 4.0960 (**−4.9%**) | **Not supported — CPC fell** |

Week-over-week, CPC **fell on both pages they named.** But 4 August alone was a genuine
product-page spike — ZAR 2.8147, the highest in 15 days — confirmed a second way by
comparing the same hours of the day across the previous week.

**Reversal 1 — the first explanation was right but useless.** It initially said the rise was
an artefact: CPC went up only because spend fell 13.19% and clicks fell 16.30%, so the
denominator shrank faster. **That was true at page level and completely hid the real thing.**

**Where the answer actually was — one level down, in the relevance caches** (the different
algorithms that decide which product to show):

| Cache | CPC 28 Jul | CPC 4 Aug | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| GRANULAR_SOLR_CATEGORY | 2.7491 | 3.2888 | **+19.6%** |
| CATEGORY_PRICE_DISCOUNT_…_L3 | 2.1940 | 2.6033 | **+18.7%** |
| CATEGORY_TOP_POPULAR_PRODUCTS | 2.4477 | 2.8317 | **+15.7%** |
| BRAND_GRANULAR_CATEGORY | 2.9644 | 3.4157 | **+15.2%** |
| SOLR_CATEGORY | 2.5225 | 2.8513 | **+13.0%** |
| SOLR_CATEGORY_REMARKETING | 2.2752 | 2.2002 | −3.3% |
| GRANULAR_SOLR_CATEGORY_REMARKETING | 2.3598 | 2.1472 | −9.0% |
| NOVELTY_CATEGORY_CACHE | 4.1953 | 3.2586 | **−22.3%** |

**Every cache that rose is a non-remarketing category cache. Both remarketing versions
fell.** A change that hits one group and spares its own close relatives is narrow and
deliberate — not general market competition.

**Nobody could have done this manually.** Bidding on these caches is **100% automated** —
across 4,289 campaigns and 1,729 merchants there are **zero manual bids.** Advertisers
cannot have raised bids 14–20% overnight; the system sets them.

**Reversal 2 — it killed its own best theory.** The obvious answer was that someone raised
the price floor. But the bid distribution had the wrong shape: the very lowest bids were
**identical to five decimal places on both days**, while the middle and upper range rose
12–17%. A floor raise pushes the *bottom* up. This did the opposite. It confirmed
separately that the ZAR 1.50 floor was unchanged and that **57.98% of product-page clicks
land exactly on it** — that count was flat while every *extra* click came in above the floor.

**It then held the mix fixed three different ways before believing the rise was real:**

| Held fixed | Result | Coverage |
|---|---|---|
| Campaign | +7.7% | 57% |
| **Merchant** | **+11.4%** | **82%** |
| Merchant × category (879 cells) | **+11.2%** | 68% |

It also worked out *why* keying on campaign was the wrong choice: **takealot campaign IDs
churn 58% per week** — the same re-creation behaviour ticket 13 found in one seller. Re-keying
on merchant pushed coverage from 57% to 82%.

**It dated the only platform change to the hour.** Hourly data shows one cache going
**233 → 0** clicks and its replacement going **195 → 467** between 13:00 and 14:00 on
**3 August** — a cache retirement finishing one day before the spike. It was volume-neutral,
so a contributor rather than the cause.

**Its conclusion:** the migration changed what inventory was available, and the automatic
bidders re-priced upward over the next ~24 hours. The rise **ramps** across three days rather
than stepping at one moment — a config change steps, an adaptive bidder ramps.

**Then it sized the problem and said it was small:**

| | |
|---|---|
| Excess cost | **≈ ZAR 5,273/day** |
| Share of marketplace PLA spend | **0.57%** |
| Marketplace CPC on 4 Aug | **below** its own 30-day average |

**And it named a much bigger problem than the ticket.** Marketplace-wide CTR is down
**8.2–10.2% on matched weekdays**, on rising impressions. **That affects 100% of spend, not
4.8%** — and nobody had raised a ticket about it. The agent wrote: *"the alarming thing isn't
the CPC increase — it's that the detection worked only because one client happened to look
at one page."*

**Reversal 7 — it overturned its own strongest piece of evidence.** It had held up merchant
EcoFlow as its best example: budgets cut, yet spending 14× more at +31.7% CPC. Asked to drill
into it:

| Cut | 28 Jul | 4 Aug | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| All SKUs *(as first reported)* | 3.8513 | 5.0732 | **+31.7%** |
| **Only SKUs present on both days** (17) | **4.5031** | **4.2630** | **−5.3%** |
| New SKUs (5, absent on 28 Jul) | — | 7.9329 | — |

For the products actually served on both days, **CPC fell.** The entire rise was five new
SKUs entering at a high price. The agent wrote: *"EcoFlow was a poorly chosen exemplar —
decompose before labelling a single merchant as evidence."* It also found the merchant's real
problem was never CPC at all: **their wallet fell 64.5% in seven days.**

**What it admitted it could not do:**

| Limitation | Impact |
|---|---|
| The bid column meanings are **inferred** | It searched all 43 configs, found no definition, and said so rather than asserting |
| The CATEGORY page was never drilled to cache level | Incomplete |
| **No page-level ROAS exists anywhere** | So "did the advertisers paying 13–20% more actually convert?" — the first thing a client asks — **could not be answered at all** |

## 4. What we learned across all of them

**6 of 15 tickets described something that was not happening** (1 partly, 2, 3, 4, 12, 13),
and a seventh was half wrong (15). Of the rest, most turned out to be advertiser setup or
mismatched expectations. **Only 3 pointed at something on our side:** Apollo's untagged
requests (8), TIRA's unfillable requests (14), and takealot's cache re-pricing (15).

| Lesson | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Check the denominator first** | Response rate fell because requests rose. CTR fell because impressions rose. CPC "rose" because cheap clicks disappeared. In every case the top half of the fraction was fine |
| **But don't stop there** | Ticket 15 is the counter-example — the denominator answer was *correct and still wrong*. Underneath it, five caches had a real 13–20% price rise the page average hid. The rule is **decompose until the explanation stops changing** |
| **Compare like with like** | Ticket 15's headline swung from +14.7% to +1.2% purely on which baseline was chosen. Measuring a Tuesday against a weekend makes an ordinary move look like an incident |
| **"A competitor is doing this" usually means "we're doing this"** | Self-competition appeared twice (1, 4). On ticket 4 the rival setting the price was their own sibling campaign at ₹300+ against an external floor of ₹11–15 |
| **New campaigns need time** | Appeared twice (12, 13), both judged in the first week, both from looking at a live search page instead of a report |
| **The same advertiser raised two invalid tickets in three days** | Tickets 3 and 12, both from eyeballing a page. Flagged for the account team |

## 5. What it does when it can't answer

**This is the section I'd demo if there were only time for one thing.** An agent that
invents an answer is worse than no agent.

| Situation | What it did |
|---|---|
| A campaign had budget but no spend | Checked four preconditions, found **zero products attached**, and stopped: *"you cannot lose an auction you never entered."* It explicitly skipped the keyword, category and competition steps |
| Asked to rank the worst-performing keywords | The report returns rows alphabetically and caps at 100,000 — so it could only see keywords starting with "6". Rather than rank that slice, it measured the gap (**143,733 of 16.5M requests — 0.9%, and not random**) and answered at category level instead, where the data was complete |
| A report returned zero rows | Instead of concluding "not delivering", it re-ran the same report against a campaign known to be spending. That proved the report was healthy and the emptiness was real. **On ticket 12 this control is the only reason the answer wasn't wrong** |
| Asked for a top-merchants table | The biggest single merchant was **1.1%** of the affected spend across 1,729. It said the ranking would be padding — **and refused to produce it** |
| A published answer was contradicted | Revised it and **struck through** the old numbers instead of quietly deleting them (ticket 10) |
| Told "you're wrong, look at 15 days" | Pulled the wider window, **confirmed the user was right**, then found **its own corrected version was also wrong** — it had included a partial day. It proved why partial days mislead and withdrew that claim too |

**It also found three bugs in our own tooling:**

| Bug | How it was caught |
|---|---|
| A report silently ignored a client filter | Returned all 7,805 marketplace rows for a single-client query. The agent noticed the row count |
| `daily_budget = 0` actually means "no row for that day" | Proven because utilisation still read 100%. This is the difference between reporting a −18.2% and a −4.6% change |
| Our MCP layer dropped data while claiming it hadn't | 1,000 returned rows held only **30.8%** of true spend. Found by reconciling against an independent total. Fixed the same day |

| Gap | Consequence |
|---|---|
| No readable keyword→category mapping | Tickets 1, 3 and 12 all asked about it. It can only be inferred from which cache served a product, never listed |

## 7. Three things I'd tell the team

**1. Every defect came from running the agent, never from reading it.**
13 defects across 11 runs. The static checks — valid column names, report types exist,
generated docs in sync — passed cleanly the entire time and caught **none** of them. The real
failures were things like an invented data-retention limit, a check that stopped on the wrong
condition, and budget semantics documented backwards. **Any review of a future runbook has to
include actually running it.**

**2. When the same bug appears twice, stop fixing instances and go looking.**
One bug — a query filtering on a date column that wasn't the one the table is organised by,
so nothing got skipped — appeared **four times**. I found three by accident, then swept for
it deliberately and found the fourth. Result: **~277 GB → ~38 GB** scanned per full pass, with
identical data. The lesson is the sweep, not the fix.

**3. A near-100% response rate can be a warning sign, not good news.**
Three takealot ad units sat at 99.99% fill and then collapsed. I diagnosed a platform failure
and recommended escalating it. **I was wrong.** A house campaign bidding almost nothing had
been winning every auction, and it simply hit its scheduled end date. ZAR 1,782 of filler was
answering 2.4 million requests. **The 99.99% was the warning.** I had checked whether the
departed campaigns' *spend* mattered; the right question was whether they could *fill*.

**One more, on trusting tickets.** 6 of 15 were outright invalid and most of the rest had a
wrong premise, a wrong agency, a wrong ID type or a misread table. In several cases the
reporter had looked at a live search page rather than a report. **There is a reporting or
expectation-setting gap upstream of engineering** that's worth a conversation with the
account teams.

**And the honest note on ticket 15.** It ran to twenty-odd follow-ups and the agent reversed
itself seven times, including on its own strongest evidence. Every reversal is logged with
what caused it. That's the behaviour I want — but it carries a warning: **the first three
answers were each defensible and each incomplete.** Anyone who stopped at one of them would
have shipped something wrong. **The agent does not know when it has gone deep enough. A human
still decides that** — on ticket 15 it was the user pushing to widen the window that forced
the drill which found the real mechanism.

**On readiness, plainly:** the reports are done and reviewed. Against test it works, and it
has produced 15 usable answers. Production waits on the deployment steps in section 6. I
wouldn't soften that in front of anyone deciding whether to depend on it.

## 8. Suggested demo run

Six minutes, a real anomaly, and a clean story:

```
For agency 105, find a PLA campaign (campaign_type = PERFORMANCE) with budget but low
spend 2026-08-01..02, then diagnose why it isn't spending.
```
