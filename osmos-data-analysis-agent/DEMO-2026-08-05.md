## 1. What this is

A Claude Code agent that works marketplace ad-performance tickets. It reads a runbook per
problem type, pulls governed reports, does the arithmetic itself, and checks in at each
step rather than producing a finished essay.

**It never writes SQL.** Every report is a reviewed SQL template frozen in kamService. The
agent chooses *which columns, which dates, which filters* — nothing else. 43 reports, all
peer-reviewed in a PR. It picks from a menu it cannot alter.

```
you ask a question in plain language
      ↓
Claude Code — 11 runbooks; ONE triggers by matching the request
      ↓  MCP
reporting server — run_report(reportType, attributes, metrics, dateRanges, filters)
      ↓  HTTPS
kamService → BigQuery
```

### Ticket 1 — "Active campaign not serving ads" · vegolution · bigbasket

### Ticket 2 — "Low CTR on PLA custom, category & home page" (#118296) · apollo

**Given:** CTR down across three page types, March vs June. Ticket quoted agency 392
(wrong marketplace).

**Pulled:** `PAGE_PERFORMANCE_PLA_REPORT`, `CATEGORY_PERFORMANCE_REPORT`,
`MERCHANT_PERFORMANCE_REPORT`.

**Found — impression dilution, no pipeline defect.**

| Page | Mar imp | Jun imp | Imp Δ | Clicks Δ | Mar CTR | Jun CTR | I/R Mar→Jun |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| PRODUCT | 336,496 | 2,718,392 | **+708%** | +23.6% | 5.96% | 0.91% | **13.8% → 98.1%** |
| HOME | 305,099 | 1,081,181 | +254% | **−34.5%** | 1.48% | 0.27% | 3.0% → 6.4% |
| CATEGORY | 181,592 | 366,440 | +102% | +17.6% | 2.46% | 1.43% | 14.8% → 28.2% |
| CUSTOM | 2,773,143 | 3,067,623 | +10.6% | −46.9% | 0.611% | 0.293% | 57.3% → 75.1% |
| SEARCH | 4,613,264 | 4,327,115 | −6.2% | −13.3% | 1.490% | 1.377% | 84.5% → 69.9% |
| **TPA** (new) | — | 313,312 | new | new | — | **6.92%** | — |
| **TOTAL** | 8,209,594 | 11,874,063 | **+44.6%** | **+7.5%** | 1.397% | 1.038% | |

**The I/R gate answered the client's actual question.** I/R rose on every complained page —
PRODUCT went 13.8% → 98.1%. In March about 1 in 7 responses rendered as an impression; in
June nearly all do. Duplicate counting would push I/R above 100%; it sits at 98.1%. **No
pipeline defect, no duplicate signals.**

Three compounding causes, all on Apollo's side:

1. **Product-page rendering change** — the I/R jump above.
2. **Catalog re-categorisation.** "Health Devices" vanished (86,348 → 0 impressions);
   "Medical Equipments & Devices" appeared. June adds a clinical taxonomy absent in March

   Oropharynx" and "Ear, Nose & Oropharynx" are separate rows with identical values.
3. **Advertiser roster turnover.** New merchants below average CTR: 1,045,910 impressions
   at **0.61%**. Churned merchants above average: 464,949 impressions / 18,412 clicks at
   **3.96% — 16% of all March clicks, gone.**

CENTRUM alone accounts for +749,612 impressions ≈ 20% of the total increase, with CTR
halved. TPA is the best surface on the marketplace and *lifts* blended CTR.

### Ticket 4 — "Centrum Ostocalcium keyword CPC issue" · apollo

**Given:** a comparison table implying competitors were driving up CPC on "centrum".

**Found — invalid premise. All four rows in the ticket's table belong to Centrum itself.**

| Ticket row | What it actually is |
|---|---|
| Centrum EXACT · ₹304.42 | Centrum's own brand-defence campaign 1251331 |
| **Supradyn EXACT · ₹61.40** | **Centrum's own campaign bidding on "supradyn"** |
| **Revital EXACT · ₹64.70** | **Same Centrum campaign bidding on "revital"** |
| Zincovit EXACT · ₹0.00 | Same campaign, no delivery |

The table compares brand-defence CPC against conquesting CPC — different auctions, both
theirs — and compares a realised average CPC against a bid.

**What competitors actually pay on "centrum":**

| Advertiser | Imp | Clicks | Spend | CPC | Auto/Manual |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **CENTRUM** | **6,710** | **304** | **₹51,550.85** | ₹169.58 | 2,903 / 3,807 |
| HEALTHKART | 3,548 | 36 | ₹2,480.00 | ₹68.89 | 100% auto |
| Diataal | 1,893 | 13 | ₹495.00 | ₹38.08 | 100% auto |
| Supradyn | 675 | 17 | ₹679.73 | ₹39.98 | 462 / 213 |

Centrum holds **47.3% of impressions, 80.9% of clicks, 93.2% of spend**. Only four external
campaigns target "centrum" manually, at **₹11.01, ₹11.06, ₹15.00 and ₹0** per click.
**Nobody is outbidding Centrum.**

**Real root cause — self-competition, starting July.** "centrum" EXACT is targeted in
**eight** Centrum campaigns. **Six were created 6–13 July**, immediately before the ticket.
In a second-price auction the runner-up sets the price; when the runner-up is your own
sibling bidding ₹300+, you pay ₹300+. External rivals bid ₹11–15. Dropping the bid loses
the slot to a sibling — **the placement is lost to Centrum itself.**

### Ticket 5 — "Managed service performance improvement" · firstcry

**Given:** ROAS below the 2.5–5 target for two brands after optimisation work.

**Found — CUGO has no problem; Ashpveda's target is arithmetically unreachable.**

**CUGO's PLA ROAS is 2.49 — inside target.** The reported 1.79 is a blend dragged down by a
Display campaign with zero conversions (₹5,769 of spend, nothing returned).

**Ashpveda:** 115 SKUs advertised for ₹28,952 returning ₹14,373 and 33 orders.
**97 of 115 SKUs (84%) produced zero orders**, consuming ₹17,542 — **60.6% of spend** — and
2,792 clicks. Four loss-making categories took ₹10,590 (36.6% of spend) and returned ₹668,
blended ROAS **0.06**.

The optimisations *did* work — click-to-order doubled, AOV +20%, attributed CVR up. ROAS
stayed low because CPC rose 64% while CTR halved.

**The budget arithmetic, which is the real answer:**

- Restricting to every SKU at ROAS ≥ 1.0 (perfect hindsight) → **ROAS 1.92**
- Restricting to SKUs at ROAS ≥ 2.0 → **ROAS 3.33, on only ₹1,811 over five weeks**
  (~₹1,550/month = 1.5% of the ₹100K budget)
- **Ashpveda's total site revenue across all channels was ₹19,114 against ₹28,952 of ad
  spend.** Ads already drive 75% of platform revenue. ROAS 2.5 on ₹100K/month requires
  ₹250,000/month of attributed GMV — roughly **15× the brand's entire demand on FirstCry.**

**ROAS 2.5–5 and ₹100K monthly spend are mutually exclusive for this brand.** Pushing
toward the budget lowers CTR and raises CPC further.

### Ticket 6 — "New/restarted campaigns spend faster than usual" · firstcry

**Given:** hypothesis that new campaigns are prioritised and burn budget too fast —
₹10k in 4 hours.

**Found — ACCELERATED budget delivery, and the evidence reverses the hypothesis.**

`BUDGET_DELIVERY_MODE_REPORT` shows both named campaigns set to **ACCELERATED**, which
instructs the system to spend the daily budget as fast as possible with no pacing. ₹10k in
4 hours is the configured behaviour.

Then a sweep of 120 of 1,666 active PERFORMANCE campaigns, cohorted by ID as an age proxy:

| Cohort | ID range | Sampled | ACCELERATED | STANDARD | % accelerated |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Oldest** | 869,525 – 940,867 | 40 | **28** | 12 | **70.0%** |
| Middle | — | 40 | 25 | 15 | 62.5% |
| **Newest** | 1,348,954 – 1,354,670 | 40 | **0** | **40** | **0.0%** |

**Every one of the 40 newest campaigns is STANDARD. Not one is ACCELERATED.** If new
campaigns were prioritised they would be the fast spenders; the opposite holds.

**And the named campaign is not new.** Marketing id 919802 falls inside the *oldest* cohort.
It was **restarted**, not created — which is exactly why restarted campaigns show the
symptom: they carry the old ACCELERATED default.

### Ticket 11 — "80 manual-category-bid campaigns at BU ≤ 50%" · bigbasket

**Given:** an internal CSV — "Manual campaigns with BU ≤ 50%", 80 campaigns, as of 3 Aug.

**First finding: the export's "Campaign ID" column mixed two ID namespaces.** 58 of 80 rows
carried `marketing_campaign_group_id` (the 5xxxxxx values); the other 22 carried
`marketing_campaign_id` (1xxxxxx). Filtering `CAMPAIGN_LOOKUP_REPORT` on
`perf_marketing_campaign_id` **silently returned only the 22** — zero rows and no error for
the rest. The agent re-filtered the unmatched values on `perf_marketing_campaign_group_id`
and recovered them.

**Second finding: 8 of the 80 were false positives already running at 100% BU.**

**Root cause: product-selection starvation.** **47 of 80 campaigns contain exactly one SKU**
and 62 of 80 have ≤2, against four- and five-figure daily budgets. A campaign holding one
product can only win the slots where that product is the relevant answer; budget cannot
change that. Low RR in these categories is the *consequence* of thin supply, not a cause.

Demand is not the constraint. Bids are not — in the worst-affected L3s there is only one
campaign and one merchant, so there are no rivals. Wallets are not (2 of 80).

**Recoverable headroom INR 399,058/day.** Blended cohort ROAS is **38×** (46,119 spend →
1,760,687 attributed GMV) — no efficiency argument for under-delivering.

### Ticket 14 — "Low RR despite increased requests" · tira

**Given:** two windows — "July 16–19: requests increased, responses did not scale" and
"July 31: requests increased while responses reduced". Plus an `image.png` chart the agent
never received.

**First, a correction to the ticket.** Only **Jul 17–18** broke, not Jul 16–19. Jul 16
(RR 68.58%) and Jul 19 (67.88%) are normal days.

| Date | Requests | Responses | RR |
|---|---|---|---|
| Jul 16 | 867,673 | 595,037 | 68.58% ← normal |
| Jul 17 | 1,668,528 | 621,230 | **37.23%** |
| Jul 18 | 1,502,830 | 596,297 | **39.68%** |
| Jul 19 | 921,577 | 625,608 | 67.88% ← recovered |
| Jul 30 | 840,046 | 543,602 | 64.71% |
| Jul 31 | 1,434,501 | 529,233 | **36.89%** |

**Marginal fill on the surge — the decisive number:** Jul 17 added 800,855 requests and
26,193 responses = **3.3% marginal fill.** Jul 31 added 594,455 requests and **lost** 14,369
responses.

**Isolated to CUSTOM pages.** SEARCH RR was steady throughout (75.14% → 74.67%; 68.94% →
70.68%). Not a platform-wide failure.

**Budget exhaustion ruled out by the hourly cut.** RR was already **24.66% in the midnight
hour** on Jul 31 and still 23.35% at 11pm — flat-bad all day. Exhaustion produces a normal
morning and a cliff. The surge is also uniform across all 24 hours (1.71×–3.43×), including
03:00–05:00 IST when shopper traffic is at its floor — real demand does not behave that way.

**Then the root cause, by brand:** on 31 July TIRA emitted **633,205 CUSTOM ad requests
filtered to the brand `Anua` — 919× the previous day's 689 — and every single one returned
zero ads.** Anua accounts for **107.5%** of the brand-filtered request increase (over 100%
because every other brand's requests *fell* 17.9% that day) and **63% of all CUSTOM requests
that day.**

**Remove Anua and the incident does not exist:** CUSTOM RR on 31 July would have been
**60.70%**, against 30 July's **60.47%** on the same basis.

**And it is not a name-mismatch bug** — Anua *is* in tira's catalogue, with 24 SKUs. There
is simply no Anua campaign, so nothing can serve. **Still leaking ~20k requests/day.**

**Data-quality finding along the way:** 100% of TIRA CUSTOM requests carry a blank
`category_l1` — 1,005,400 of 1,005,400 on Jul 31. Category-level RR attribution is
structurally impossible for this marketplace, which is why the investigation had to go via
hourly shape and brand.

## 4. Cross-ticket patterns

**6 of 14 tickets were explicitly invalid** — the reported problem was not happening
(tickets 1 partly, 2, 3, 4, 12, 13). Of the remaining 8, most resolved to advertiser
configuration or expectation. **Only 2 pointed at something on our side:** Apollo's untagged
category requests (8) and TIRA's unfillable request volume (14).

**The recurring error is reading a metric without its denominator.** RR fell because
requests rose. CTR fell because impressions rose. CPC "rose" because cheap filler clicks
disappeared. In each case the numerator was fine.

**Self-competition appeared twice** (tickets 1 and 4) and in both cases the advertiser
believed a competitor was responsible. On ticket 4 the runner-up in the auction was the
advertiser's own sibling campaign bidding ₹300+ against an external floor of ₹11–15.

**New-campaign ramp appeared twice** (12 and 13), both judged during the first week, both
from inspecting a live search page rather than reporting. Ticket 13's seller has re-created
the same campaign eight times, restarting the ramp each time.

**Two tickets came from the same advertiser (os_client 10092920) in three days** — 3 and 12.
Both invalid, both raised from eyeballing a live page. Flagged for the account team in both
logs.

## 5. How it behaves when it can't answer

This is the part I would demo if there were time for only one thing.

**It stopped at a gate rather than guessing.** On a campaign with budget but no spend, it
checked four preconditions, found zero products attached, and refused to go further —
*"you cannot lose an auction you never entered."* It explicitly skipped the keyword,
category and competition drills.

**It refused to rank data it could only partially see.** Asked for the worst-response-rate
keywords, it found the report returns rows alphabetically and caps at 100,000 — reaching
only keywords starting with `6`. Rather than rank that slice, it quantified the coverage
(**143,733 of 16.5M requests — 0.9%, and non-random**) and answered at category level
instead, where the data was complete: **81% of all lost fill sits in the blank/unmapped
`category_l1` bucket at 59.7% RR.**

**It checked its own tooling before blaming the data.** Several times a report returned zero
rows. Instead of concluding "not delivering", it re-ran against a campaign known to be
spending — proving the report healthy and the emptiness real. On ticket 12 that control is
the only reason the answer wasn't wrong.

**It revised a published conclusion** (ticket 10) when a further query contradicted it, and
struck the superseded figures through rather than quietly deleting them.

**It caught a bug in our own MCP layer** by reconciling a 1,000-row result against an
independent total: 1,000 rows carried only **30.8%** of true spend while the response
claimed nothing had been dropped. Fixed the same day.

## 6. What is still missing

### Blocking production

| Gap | Impact |
|---|---|
| **174 `perf_*` columns absent from `config/columnMetadata.json`** | They resolve in test only because they were loaded straight into test's database. The file is what deploys. Until fixed, a file-based production deploy leaves every external column unresolvable. **Needs the kamService deploy owner.** |
| **`GCP_PERF_BQ_KAM_CREDENTIALS` not registered in production** | Test only. Needs infra. |

### Capability gaps

- **No keyword→category mapping is readable.** Tickets 1, 3 and 12 all asked about it. It
  can be verified indirectly from which relevance cache served a SKU, never enumerated.
- **No floor-price report.** CPC moving one way on every page type is the floor signature,
  and the agent has to ask a human to check it.
- **Attributed metrics keep settling.** Measured: the same query returned
  ZAR 11,771,088 then ZAR 11,821,990 hours apart for the same window, while the older
  baseline was byte-identical. The agent now flags this; it cannot correct for it.
- **Filters on metric columns are silently ignored** — kamService builds filters into the
  `WHERE` clause and a metric selector is an aggregate, so it is dropped with no error.
- **`sku-drilldown` sub-agent has never been exercised.** The only component with no
  runtime evidence.

### Data quality the agent surfaced — not agent problems, but the team should know

- **92.2% of Apollo category-page requests arrive untagged** (ticket 8).
- **100% of TIRA CUSTOM requests carry a blank `category_l1`** (ticket 14).
- **Apollo's taxonomy has a genuine duplicate** — "Ear Nose & Oropharynx" and "Ear, Nose &
  Oropharynx" as separate rows with identical values (ticket 2).
- **Raw Takealot URLs are landing in `category_l1`** — one hookah PLID took 260 requests at
  0% RR. Something upstream isn't parsing search input.
- **Relevance-cache names differ per marketplace.** takealot exposes six, bigbasket two.
  Any cache list is marketplace-specific.
- **TIRA is emitting ~20k unfillable Anua requests/day**, still open.

## 7. From me — for the team

**Every defect found in this work came from running the agent, not from reading the code.**
13 defects across 11 runs. Static checks — column names valid, report types exist, generated
docs in sync — passed cleanly the whole time and found **none** of them. The failures were
things like a fabricated retention limit, a gate terminating on the wrong condition, and
budget semantics documented backwards. Whatever review we apply to future runbooks has to
include running them.

**The same class of bug appeared four times.** A query bounded a timestamp column that
wasn't the table's partition key, so nothing pruned. Found by accident three times before I
swept for it systematically and found the fourth. **~277 GB → ~38 GB** per full pass, data
identical. The lesson is the sweep, not the fix — when a bug appears twice, stop fixing
instances and go looking.

**Be careful reading a near-100% response rate as health.** On takealot, three ad units sat
at 99.99% fill and then collapsed. I diagnosed a platform failure and recommended
escalation. **I was wrong** — a house campaign at CPM 1.01 had been winning every auction
and hit its scheduled end date. The 99.99% *was* the warning sign. I had tested whether the
departed campaigns' *spend* was material; the right question was whether they could *fill*.
ZAR 1,782 of filler was answering 2.4M requests.

**Trust the tickets less than the data.** 6 of 14 were outright invalid and most of the rest
had a wrong premise, a wrong agency, a wrong ID namespace or a mis-read table. In several
the reporter had looked at a live search page rather than a report. There is a reporting or
expectation-setting gap upstream of engineering worth a conversation with account teams.

**On readiness.** The reports are done and reviewed; the transport is not. Against test it
works and has produced 14 usable answers. In production, not until the `columnMetadata` and
appKey items land. I would not soften that in front of anyone deciding whether to depend
on it.

## 8. Suggested demo run

Six minutes, real anomaly, clean narrative:

```
For agency 105, find a PLA campaign (campaign_type = PERFORMANCE) with budget but low
spend 2026-08-01..02, then diagnose why it isn't spending.
Use only osmos-performance-local.
```

It picks its own subject, uses a control campaign to rule out a false negative, hits a hard
gate and stops, and hands over an answer with a named owner — *"route to onboarding, not
ads-ops bid tuning."*

**Restart the session first.** The MCP server launches at session start and there are recent
fixes in the file a running process won't have.

**Avoid:** asking for SKU drill-down (untested sub-agent) and marketplace-wide keyword
drills (slow; calls get backgrounded past the 120-second tool timeout).

**Two things that look like failures but aren't:** `CAMPAIGN_NETWORKS_REPORT` legitimately
returns nothing for takealot — that data lives in other agencies. And large fetches return a
file path plus row count rather than rows, by design, so the agent can analyse the complete
set without paying for it in context.
